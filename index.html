Solar Guide

Solar Power Explained for Madurai Homes and Businesses

Before you invest, it helps to understand how a solar system works, what it can save you, and what to check. This guide covers the basics in plain language.

How a Rooftop Solar System Works

A rooftop solar system has four main parts. Solar panels convert sunlight into direct current (DC) electricity. The DC power travels through protection devices such as DC MCBs to an inverter. The inverter converts DC into alternating current (AC), the same type of electricity your home appliances use. In a grid-tied system, any extra power your home does not use can flow into the grid, depending on your net-metering arrangement. A battery, if added, stores surplus power for use during the night or during a power cut.

Because panels generate power only when there is sunlight, the output changes through the day and across seasons. Shade, dust, panel tilt, and panel temperature all affect how much energy you actually get. A well-designed system accounts for these factors from the start.

Understanding Net-Metering

Net-metering allows a rooftop solar consumer to export surplus units to the grid and receive credit against future consumption, subject to the rules of the local distribution utility. For Madurai consumers, the process and approvals are handled through the local electricity distribution utility. Rules and procedures can change, so we confirm the current process with you before any installation.

Net-metering is the reason many homes choose a grid-tied system: it usually means you do not need a battery to benefit from solar, which keeps the initial cost lower.

Is Solar Worth It in Madurai?

Tamil Nadu receives good levels of sunshine for much of the year, which makes rooftop solar a practical option for many households and businesses. Whether it is worth it for you depends on four things: your monthly electricity consumption, how much of that consumption happens during daytime, the space and condition of your roof, and the tariff you currently pay.

Households with higher daytime usage, such as those running air conditioners, water pumps, or home businesses during the day, often see the fastest return. Commercial premises that operate in daylight hours are also strong candidates.

Solar System Size: A Simple Way to Estimate

A common rough estimate is to divide your average monthly consumption (in units or kWh) by 30 to get the average daily consumption, then divide that by roughly 4 to get an approximate system size in kilowatts (kW). For example, a home using about 300 units per month may start with a system in the range of 2 to 3 kW, depending on the load pattern and roof space.

This is only a starting estimate. Our site visit checks your load pattern, roof area and shade so the final recommendation is based on real conditions.

What Affects Your Savings?

  • Your daytime electricity usage and how much you can shift to daytime hours
  • Roof direction, tilt, and shade from trees or nearby buildings
  • Quality of panels, inverter, and cabling
  • Regular cleaning and timely maintenance
  • Current tariff slabs and any applicable government support

Government Support and Subsidies

Central government schemes for rooftop solar, such as the PM Surya Ghar: Muft Bijli Yojana, may offer subsidies for eligible residential households. Eligibility criteria, subsidy amounts, and the application process are set by the authorities and can change over time. Always verify the latest details on the official government portal before making a decision. We can guide you through the documents and steps when you are ready.

More Solar Questions Answered

Does solar work on cloudy days?

Yes, solar panels still produce some power on cloudy days, though output is lower than on clear sunny days.

How long do solar panels last?

Most panels come with long performance warranties, and they typically continue producing power for decades, with output gradually declining over time. Check the warranty terms for the specific product.

Do solar panels need a lot of maintenance?

Solar systems need relatively little maintenance. Regular cleaning of panels, periodic inspection of wiring and connections, and checking the inverter's performance are the main tasks.

Will rain or dust reduce my output?

Rain naturally cleans panels, but dust and bird droppings can reduce output over time. Periodic cleaning helps maintain performance.

Can I add a battery later?

In many cases yes, provided the inverter supports battery connection. Plan for this at the design stage to avoid rework later.

What happens to my system if the power goes out?

Grid-tied systems without a battery shut down during outages for the safety of line workers. Hybrid systems with batteries can provide backup power for selected loads.
