An on-grid inverter works only when grid power is available and focuses on bill savings. A hybrid inverter also manages battery storage, giving you backup during outages.

Buying Guide

How to Choose the Right Solar Inverter

Step 1: Decide Your Goal

Start with the main reason you are buying. If you want to cut your monthly bill, an on-grid or a grid-tied hybrid system is usually enough. If you want backup during power cuts, you need a hybrid inverter with battery support. If your location has little or no grid supply, an off-grid inverter with batteries is the right category.

Step 2: Match the Capacity to Your Load

Inverter capacity is usually expressed in kVA or kW. Add up the power ratings of the appliances you want to run at the same time, then allow some margin for motor start-up loads such as pumps and air conditioners. An inverter that is too small will struggle under peak load, while one that is far too large can cost more than necessary.

Step 3: Check Battery Compatibility

The battery voltage (for example 12 V, 24 V or 48 V), battery chemistry, and maximum charge current must match the inverter's specifications. Mismatched batteries can reduce life and create safety risks, so we always confirm compatibility before recommending a combination.

Step 4: Look at Input Voltage and Protection

Madurai's grid voltage can fluctuate. A good inverter should have a wide input voltage range and protection features such as overload, short-circuit, over-temperature and low-battery cut-off. Check these specifications on the datasheet, not only on the brochure.

Step 5: Think About Future Expansion

If you may add more panels or a battery later, choose an inverter with headroom or one that supports expansion. This avoids replacing the inverter when your needs grow.

Know Your Parts

Batteries and Solar Panels Explained

Battery Types

Tubular lead-acid: Established technology with a lower upfront cost. Needs periodic water top-up and suits moderate backup needs.

Lithium-ion (LiFePO4): Higher upfront cost but longer cycle life, lighter weight, and no water top-up. Often chosen for high-use backup systems.

Battery performance and warranty terms vary by brand, so we compare them against your backup needs.

Solar Panel Types

Monocrystalline: Made from single-crystal silicon. Generally offers higher efficiency per square metre, which helps where roof space is limited.

Polycrystalline: Made from multiple silicon crystals. Usually a lower cost per panel, with slightly lower efficiency.

We recommend panels based on roof area, budget and the warranty offered by the manufacturer.

Before You Buy

Checklist for Any Solar Product Purchase

  • Ask for the datasheet and check the actual specifications
  • Confirm warranty period and what it covers, in writing
  • Find out who handles warranty claims and where service is located
  • Check that components are compatible with each other
  • Compare quotations item by item, not only the total price
  • Verify that the product certifications and standards are stated clearly
Product FAQ

More Product Questions

Can an inverter run an air conditioner?

Yes, if the inverter capacity and battery bank are sized for the AC's starting and running load. AC systems need careful planning because compressors draw high start-up current.

How many fans and lights can a small inverter run?

This depends on the inverter rating and the battery backup time you need. We calculate the load based on your actual appliances.

Do solar panels need a specific mounting structure?

Yes. The structure depends on the roof type, wind load, and panel orientation. We select mounting that is safe and suitable for your building.

Can I buy only the inverter and install it myself?

We strongly recommend professional installation. Solar systems involve DC high-voltage circuits, earthing, and grid connection requirements that need trained technicians.
